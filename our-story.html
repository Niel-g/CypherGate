About CypherGate

We didn't build CypherGate because it was a good business idea. We built it because we watched a ransomware attack destroy everything a friend worked for — and we refused to let that happen again.

Where It All Began

A Ransomware Attack That Changed Everything

In early 2025, a group of young technology professionals in the Philippines watched helplessly as a friend's growing e-commerce business was crippled by ransomware. The attack was devastating — three weeks offline, thousands of pesos lost, and a customer base shaken to its core.

The hardest part? The breach exploited a vulnerability that had been publicly known for over six months. A simple patch would have prevented everything. There was no sophisticated attack. No nation-state threat actor. Just a known gap that no one had closed — because the business had no one watching.

That moment became the founding spark. Mariah Sumicad, then leading her thesis on enterprise security architecture, gathered her closest collaborators and posed a single question:

"Why should cybersecurity be complicated and exclusive when it doesn't have to be?"

— Mariah Sumicad, CEO & Co-Founder

Building the Company

From Thesis Project to Full Security Startup

What began as a thesis project and a weekend hackathon entry quickly evolved into a structured startup. The founding team spent months studying how enterprise security firms operated, dissecting what made them effective, and identifying exactly what smaller businesses were missing.

The answer was never just software — it was the people, the process, and the around-the-clock attention that software alone could never provide. A firewall doesn't call you at 3 AM. An automated scanner doesn't make the judgment call between a real threat and a false positive. Humans do.

They built CypherGate on a single foundational philosophy: Security-as-a-Service. Not a one-time tool you install and forget, but a living, breathing external security department that monitors, defends, and recovers on behalf of its clients — every hour of every day.

They named the company CypherGate to represent the gateway between vulnerability and protection — between the chaos of the digital world and the clarity every business deserves.
